Points

A raid pays the price of the clan you hit, as it stands when the flag comes down — {{RAID_VALUE_BASE|n}} for a quiet established clan, more for one that is out raiding, less for one that was just hit, nothing for a clan under {{NEW_CLAN_WORTHLESS_MS|days}} old. Hit someone while a member is online and it pays {{RAID_ONLINE_MULT}}; nobody home, {{RAID_OFFLINE_MULT}}. The full rules are in Raiding.

Points are banked. Once a raid pays you, those points are yours for the season. Getting raided never takes any away; it only lowers your price for a while.

Your rank is your season points. If two clans tie, the one raided fewer times this season ranks higher; if still tied, the older clan does.

What a raid pays right now

The top of #scoreboard and the site's scoreboard list every active clan with the price a raid on it would pay at this moment, highest first. Beside each price:

  • 🔥 how many raids the clan has landed in the last {{RAID_HEAT_FADE_MS|days}}
  • 🩹 how many times it has been hit in the last {{RAID_HIT_FADE_MS|days}}
  • 🐣 for a new clan, the date it starts counting
  • 🟢 someone from the clan is online, so the price shown includes {{RAID_ONLINE_MULT}}. Without the 🟢 the price shown includes {{RAID_OFFLINE_MULT}}.

The price on the board is the price the next raid pays. Dormant clans are listed as can't be raided.

What the server can see

Who is online comes from the same connect and disconnect log as the online board, so it is already public. The board never says where anyone is.

The week

{{ALPHA_WEEK}} — the week closes as the raid window closes. At the close, the top three by points scored that week are the Alphas until the next close (or however many clans scored, if fewer than three): a badge on the directory, the scoreboard and the clan page, an @Alpha role in Discord, and the week's result in #war-log.

The weekly weapon kit. The clan with the most points that week also wins a weapon kit for {{RAID_AWARD_DAYS|n}} days, granted to its leader at the close. The leader can give it to a clanmate from the site, as with any award. If no clan scored that week, there is no kit. It is announced in #server-events.

The season

A season runs from one server wipe (a full reset of the game world) to the next. At season end the champion and the full final table are recorded on the Seasons page, permanently. Every clan's placement goes on its clan page. Points reset to zero.

Identities and rosters do not reset. A wipe resets the world; it does not dissolve clans. A wipe takes every pole with it, but not your base: your clan keeps its spot, and your supply kit spawns there after the wipe.

  • A clan keeps its name, tag, flag and roster, but not the flag itself: clan flags never spawn as loot, and your kit brings yours. Build a pole within {{CARRIED_ADOPT_RADIUS_M|m}} of your old base and have a member raise a white flag or your clan's flag on it. That pole is your base, with no cooldown and no confirmation step. Further away, a white flag does nothing for your clan; your own flag asks your leader to confirm a move. The {{MIN_BASE_SPACING_M|m}} rule still applies.
  • The 7-day inactivity clock runs from the wipe. Rebuild and raise inside a week or go dormant like anyone else.
  • Every pole on the fresh map gets the 7-day launch grace again.
  • A clan that was dormant at season end stays dormant, and its disband clock keeps running, until a member raises a white flag at its new base.
  • Heat and hits start fresh with the season, so every established clan starts at {{RAID_VALUE_BASE|n}} again.

The wipe date is announced in advance. It is the one thing in this guide the server owner schedules.

What is public

With no login at all: the scoreboard (rank, clan, flag, points, raids, times raided, defenses, active or dormant), the Alpha history, the Seasons page, the war log, the clan directory, the flag pool (which of the 33 are taken), every clan page, and every player profile.

What is never public: anything with a coordinate, or anyone's position.

Player profiles

Every linked player has a public profile, per season and all-time:

  • Play time, sessions, last seen
  • PvP kills, PvP deaths, K/D, best killstreak, longest kill, and who — killed-by and killed lists, names only
  • Friendly-fire kills and friendly-fire deaths, counted separately and nowhere else
  • Raid credits (flags lowered) and upkeep raises (own flag at own pole)
  • Build points (one per build step)
  • Clan history — which clans, when

Player boards

Server-wide and public, ten names a board with the full list a click away: top raiders, top killers, best K/D (minimum 10 kills), best killstreak (PvP kills in a row without being killed by a player), longest kill, most bounty kills, top builders, most play time, and then the two nobody wants to lead — most PvP deaths and most friendly fire. Inside a clan, the same columns filtered to your roster, so you can see who is carrying, who keeps shooting Dave, and who actually built the walls.

Friendly fire counts on one board and nowhere else. Shooting a clanmate earns you nothing: not a kill, not a streak, not a point of K/D. It costs them nothing either, so a teamkill cannot dent their deaths or their ratio. The only place it shows up is the friendly-fire board and the friendly-fire counters on both profiles.

Kills are stats, never points. The season is decided by clan raids alone. If you want to win the season, raid.

What the server can see

Kills, deaths and who was involved come from the log. Whether a kill was friendly fire is decided by whether killer and victim were on the same roster at that moment. Play time is measured from connect to disconnect. A kill's distance and weapon are on the log line itself. A streak counts PvP kills in a row and ends only when another player outside your clan kills you — friendly fire neither adds nor ends one, on your profile, in the killstreak channel and for achievements alike. A build point is one build step the log records — a kit placed, a frame, a panel, a gate, a watchtower floor — at any pole, flat, and dismantling earns nothing.
